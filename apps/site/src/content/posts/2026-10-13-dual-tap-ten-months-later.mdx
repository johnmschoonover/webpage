**TL;DR:** Ten months ago I wrote up the [dual-tap setup](/case-studies/the-dual-tap-strategy-hardening-a-self-hosted-homelab-with-zeek-and-wazuh/) that watches my homelab's edge and core. The taps haven't moved. Behind them, I replaced Wazuh with Elastic and Fleet-managed agents, outgrew my VM disks after a bandwidth upgrade, and learned how much running Elastic on an internal certificate authority really costs.

## Quick recap

Two physical taps give me a before-and-after view of my network. Tap 1 sits between the ISP modem and the router and sees the internet's background noise. Tap 2 sits between the router and the core switch and sees what actually gets in.

Zeek watches both taps and turns raw packets into structured logs. When I wrote the case study, a local Wazuh agent shipped those logs for correlation. That's the part that changed.

## More bandwidth, more logs, full disks

Zeek writes a record for every connection it sees, so log volume follows traffic. When I upgraded my internet connection, my logs grew with it. Storage sized for the old link didn't last.

If you run Elasticsearch on tight disks, know its defaults. At 85% disk use it stops assigning most new shards to a node. At 90% it starts moving shards off. At 95%, the flood stage, it marks every index on that node read-only, and ingest stops. Those thresholds are reasonable on a big cluster. On a single homelab VM they're three steps from an outage.

## Running Elastic in an internal PKI world

Elastic 8 turns on TLS by default, both between Elasticsearch nodes and on its HTTP API. That's the right call. With a private certificate authority, though, trust has to line up in a lot of places at once:

- **Elasticsearch**, on both the HTTP layer (clients, Kibana, agents) and the transport layer (node to node).
- **Kibana**, both to Elasticsearch and to your browser.
- **Fleet Server's own certificate**, which every agent checks when it enrolls and every time it checks in.
- **Every Elastic Agent**, which has to trust your CA for Fleet Server and for the Elasticsearch output it ships data to.
- **Names.** Every hostname or IP an agent uses has to appear in the certificate's subject alternative names.
- **Expiry dates.** When a certificate expires, every component that relies on it fails at once.

## What I'd tell someone building this

{/* JOHN: Keep, cut, or rewrite these. They're drafted from what you told me. */}

1. **Size storage for the pipe you'll have, not the one you have.** Log volume follows bandwidth.
2. **Treat certificates as dependencies with expiry dates.** Track them like any other dependency, and automate renewal before you need it.
3. **Watch the pipeline, not just the data.** A tap that goes silent looks a lot like a quiet network.
